# 1. Introduction and Problem Background

Student Representative Council (SRC) elections at the University of Ghana, along with hall and departmental elections, are still largely conducted through manual or semi-manual processes: physical queues at polling stations, paper ballots or basic online forms with weak identity checks, hand-counted or spreadsheet-tallied results, and result announcements that candidates and observers cannot independently verify. This creates recurring problems: long queues that suppress turnout, disputed results with no verifiable audit trail, risk of ballot stuffing or double voting, and delays in collating results across halls and colleges.

This project proposes a Blockchain-Based Electronic Voting System that digitises voter verification and ballot casting while using a permissioned blockchain ledger to make every vote tamper-evident, independently auditable, and transparently countable in real time — without exposing how any individual voted.

## 1.1 Systems Theory Framing

Before modelling the solution, the problem environment was analysed using Systems Theory, viewing the election process as a system made up of inputs, processes, outputs, boundaries, and feedback loops. This framing helped the team see voter turnout, disputed results, and delayed collation not as isolated complaints but as symptoms of a system with weak boundaries (no reliable way to confirm a voter is who they claim to be) and a broken feedback loop (no verifiable receipt or audit trail feeding confidence back to voters and candidates).
